# FFCWS harmonized sexual-intercourse analysis: independent aggregate review

**Review date:** 24 November 2026.  
**Data sources:** Nine user-provided aggregate CSVs/JSON/Markdown from the local FFCWS Year-15-to-Year-22 script; the respondent-level ICPSR data were **not** transferred or accessed.  
**Reproduction:** Run `python validate_aggregates.py` with the included `inputs/`. The validation script checks internal arithmetic only; it cannot independently reproduce results from restricted respondent-level microdata.

## Main conclusions

1. Among the **same 696** weighted complete cases who reported no sexual intercourse in the Year-15 wave, **80.26%** reported any sex and **75.31%** reported vaginal intercourse at the Year-22 interview. The paired difference is **4.95 percentage points** (33-replicate jackknife 95% CI **1.51–8.40** pp). Thus the broader any-sex question overstates the more NSFG-like vaginal outcome, but the majority finding for the follow-up *status* remains.
2. For the larger vaginal-outcome-specific complete-case sample (**n=697**), the Year-15-weighted estimate was **75.30%** (95% CI **67.89–82.71%**). Baseline IPW gives **74.96%** (95% CI **67.98–81.94%**). The Year-22-weighted complete-case estimate was **74.26%** (95% CI **66.49–82.04%**). Year-15 national weight valid target n=938, age-15 vaginal outcomes observed n=697; no dedicated longitudinal survey weight is available.
3. Assigning **no vaginal intercourse to all missing adult-status observations** yields a survey-weighted lower *point* bound of **57.9857%**, with a 33-replicate 95% uncertainty interval **49.7428–66.2286%**. Assigning yes to all missing yields **80.9798%**. Thus the lower *point* bound exceeds half, but its 95% CI reaches below 50%; neither bound accounts for missing Year-15 responses or survey measurement error.
4. **Critically, a strict longitudinal initiation claim is not identical to vaginal experience by the follow-up.** Among the originally Year-15-no cohort, the adult retrospective first-vaginal-intercourse age is **definitely earlier** than the age at the Year-15 interview in **49 cases**, representing **3.9442% of the Year-15 weighted analysis population**. In **52 further cases** (4.1226% weighted), debut age equals completed age at Year-15 interview, so initiation may have been before or after the first interview. These are cross-wave timing inconsistencies or ambiguities; the data do not reveal which interview/response is accurate. A conservative **arithmetic** stress test reclassifying the 49 definite chronology conflicts as *not proven to newly initiate* reduces the missing-no lower point bound to **54.04%**. Also reclassifying all 52 same-age ambiguous cases reduces it to **49.92%**. These subtractions are not a corrected population estimate or formal partial-identification interval; resolving baseline eligibility might require changing the denominator. They show that the stronger claim of an unassailable majority **newly initiating between the two interviews** is **not** established without additional assumptions.
5. Fixed-birthday estimates use the correct young-adult respondent age `ck7yagem` rather than the respondent's child's age: **42.40% before 18** (95% CI 32.59–52.21), **69.25% before 20** (59.55–78.95), **70.93% before 21** (62.17–79.68), and **79.51% before 22** (69.95–89.07). The first three share an attained-age-eligible frame n=725 with 685 observed event-history statuses. The 22nd-birthday estimate comes from a **different selected frame** n=451 (422 observed). Do not interpret 79.51 minus 70.93 percentage points as an initiation increment during age 21. Wave title 'Year 22' does not imply all respondents had turned 22.
6. The Year-15 interview age was <15 years for **179/938** target respondents (weighted 29.87% of those with known adolescent interview age), 15–<16 for 617, 16–<17 for 123, 17+ for 19. Restricted to the **617** respondents interviewed at 15–<16, the vaginal-intercourse estimate remained **76.70%** (n=450; 95% CI 67.75–85.65). Do not describe the entire sample as precisely age 15.

## Definitions and questionnaire audit

- Year 15: `k6f19` asks whether the teen had *sexual intercourse* with their current romantic partner; `k6f26` asks about lifetime intercourse with anyone for other routes. The scripted composite treats `F19=1` or `F26=1` as a yes, and `F26=2` with `F19!=1` as a no. The question does **not** explicitly define the act as penile-vaginal intercourse. Reconfirm skip paths in the Year-15 questionnaire before publication.
- Year 22: `k7e58` asks whether the respondent ever had sex; `k7e59` separately asks about **vaginal sexual intercourse**. Respondents saying no to `E58` skip `E59`; these cases are valid no-vaginal outcomes. `k7e60` records completed integer age at first vaginal intercourse in those reporting vaginal experience. No explicit sex of vaginal partner is specified; these estimates do **not** reproduce the NSFG's male intercourse-with-woman definition exactly.
- Source questionnaires: https://ffcws.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf4356/files/ff_teen_q15.pdf (Year 15 F19/F26) and https://ffcws.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf4356/files/documents/FFCWS%20Y22%20YOUNG%20ADULT%20SURVEY_0.pdf (Year 22 E58–E60).
- The national survey weights represent births in large US cities in 1998–2000, **not** all American adolescents and **not** the 2019–2025 YRBS adolescent cohorts. The two waves use separate wave-specific response adjustments; results remain subject to attrition and residual model dependence.

## Analysis-quality caveats

- **Timing contradictions are substantive, not necessarily coding mistakes.** The 49 definite contradictions could reflect retrospective recall error, initial underreporting, changed definitions, or data processing issues. The present aggregate files do not identify the cause. The 52 same-completed-age cases are timing ambiguous rather than contradictions.
- **Survey design CIs are conditional on reported outcomes.** The 33-replicate estimates do not capture uncertainty about which conflicting history is true or about assumptions assigned to missing follow-up outcomes.
- **Observed percentages are not causal evidence.** The cohort's high early-adult experience rate cannot identify reasons for delayed intercourse, voluntariness, or later life outcomes.
- **Birthday estimates are not a Kaplan–Meier curve.** The 22-year-old milestone subgroup and the 21-year-old milestone subgroup differ by attained interview age; this can produce selection effects.
- **The Year 22 age-at-first outcome is retrospective.** Among those reporting vaginal experience, 514 cases lie in the eligible Year-15-weighted domain, 503 report plausible debut age and 11 lack valid age. The age-specific breakdown includes conflicting retrospective histories.
- **Suppressed 23rd-birthday estimates** in `birthday_milestones.csv` are intentionally omitted from scientific interpretation because of very small samples.

## Publication-calibrated takeaway

> Among male participants in the Future of Families and Child Wellbeing Study who reported no sexual intercourse at an adolescent interview, approximately 75% of linked, survey-weighted respondents reported vaginal intercourse by their young-adult interview, compared with approximately 80% reporting any sexual experience. The paired difference was 5.0 percentage points (95% CI 1.5–8.4). A pessimistic assumption that none of the respondents with missing follow-up outcomes had experienced vaginal intercourse leaves a 58% lower point bound, but its confidence interval includes 50%. Moreover, retrospective reports for a nontrivial subset indicate an intercourse debut before, or ambiguously close to, the initial interview, which limits precision about *new* initiation between interviews. Together, these data support substantial sexual experience by early adulthood in this historical urban birth cohort, while leaving the exact transition rate and its generalizability to newer cohorts uncertain.

## Recommended next verification

Perform one targeted **on-device timeline-consistency/eligibility audit** (no respondent records exported). Produce national-weighted age-15 F19/F26 route tables crossed with Year-22 retrospective first-vaginal-age relation to adolescent age (definitely-before / same-year / definitely-after / missing). Estimate initiation only among those with strictly post-teen reported age, and an interval treating same-year cases as unknown; report both year-15 and year-22 weighted sensitivity. This would directly investigate the dominant remaining uncertainty rather than repeatedly changing the IPW model.
